What Is a PIFRA Vendor Number — and Why Does It Matter?

Government offices do not only pay salaries. They also pay suppliers: the firm that delivered stationery, the contractor who repaired a building, the company that maintains office vehicles. In the government's accounting system, none of those payments can move until the payee exists as a recognised record — and that record is identified by a vendor number. If you supply goods or services to a government office in Pakistan, or you process bills in one, this number will cross your desk sooner or later. Here is what it is, in plain words.

A vendor number, defined simply

A vendor number is a unique identification number assigned in the government financial system to a person, firm, or organisation that receives payments from the government other than salary. Think of it as the system's address for a payee. When a bill is prepared, the vendor number tells the accounting software whose record to charge the payment against, which bank and tax details belong to that payee, and where the payment history should be stored. Without it, a bill has nowhere to land, and it simply waits.

The detailed walkthrough in the PIFRA vendor number guide covers how the number is used inside bill processing; this page focuses on the questions vendors and office staff ask most often.

Vendor number is not a personnel number

Personnel number: identifies a government employee and is used for salary, pay slips, and service records.
Vendor number: identifies a payee or supplier and is used for bills and non-salary payments.
Some people hold both — for example, an employee who also receives a payment in a separate capacity — but the two records are different, and using one in place of the other is a common reason bills get stuck.

Who actually needs a vendor number?

Suppliers and contractors who bill government offices are the obvious group. But vendor numbers also appear in everyday office work: payments to individuals for services, refunds processed through the accounts system, and payments to organisations that are not on any payroll. If an accounts clerk tells you that your payment "needs a vendor number first", it means your payee record has to be created or located in the system before the bill can be entered — the request is procedural, not a rejection of your claim.

Where can you find an existing vendor number?

Start with paper you already have. Vendor numbers commonly appear on earlier payment documents: previous bills passed for the same office, payment vouchers, and correspondence from the accounts office. The Drawing and Disbursing Officer of the office you supplied can also confirm the number recorded against your name or firm, because that office works with it whenever your bills are prepared. If you have dealt with more than one government office, do not assume a single story — ask the office whose bill is pending, and use the number they hold for you in their records. Employees comparing related identifiers can also consult detailed PIFRA resources to keep vendor, personnel, and DDO references separate in their own files.

What happens when the number is wrong or missing?

A wrong digit does not produce a small error; it usually produces no payment at all, because the bill either fails validation or points at the wrong payee record. A missing number means the bill cannot be entered until the record is created through the proper office channel. In both cases, the correction runs through the DDO and the concerned accounts office — not through any website. Provide your correct particulars as they appear on your official documents, and ask the office to confirm the number back to you in writing for future bills.

A safety note for employees and vendors alike: Creating or checking records in this system sometimes sits next to personal registration services, and that is where people get careless. Your CNIC and personal details belong only on the official portal, m.pifra.gov.pk — the official form fields are Government Code, Personnel Number, CNIC, date of birth, mobile number, and email, with Gmail recommended for the email field. Never enter CNIC or banking details on a guide site or a page merely claiming to "generate" a vendor number for you.

Three habits that prevent vendor number problems

First, quote your vendor number on every bill and covering letter exactly as the accounts office issued it. Second, if your firm's name, address, or bank account changes, inform the offices you supply before the next bill, not after a payment fails. Third, keep one page in your own file with your vendor number, the offices that use it, and the dates of payments received against it. When a payment is delayed, that single page turns a long inquiry into a two-minute answer: the office can trace the bill against the right record immediately.
